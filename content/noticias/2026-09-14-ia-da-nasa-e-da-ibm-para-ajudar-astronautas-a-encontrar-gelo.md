A new **artificial-intelligence** tool developed in partnership between **NASA** and **IBM** could help astronauts find **water ice** and chart safe routes on the lunar surface.

The system combines orbiter data (including hydrogen maps, temperature and permanent shadow) with machine-learning models to identify regions with higher probability of accessible ice and to assess terrain risks. The goal is to support exploration of the **lunar south pole**, where permanently shadowed craters may have preserved ice for billions of years.

For Artemis missions and future bases, lunar water is a critical resource: drinking, oxygen and propellant. An AI that can prioritise landing and sampling sites reduces time and risk for crews. The tool fits the trend of using machine learning to process the growing volume of planetary data and turn scientific maps into near-real-time operational support.
