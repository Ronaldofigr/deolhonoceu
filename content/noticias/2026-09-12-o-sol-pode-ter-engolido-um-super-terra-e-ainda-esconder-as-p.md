One possible explanation is that one or more larger planets once existed and were destroyed or swallowed by the young Sun, or ejected by gravitational interactions.

If a super-Earth had fallen into the Sun, some of its material could have mixed into the star’s outer layers. That would leave subtle **chemical signatures** — for example an anomalous enrichment of refractory elements in the photosphere — or dynamical imprints on the architecture of the remaining planets.

Detecting those signatures is extremely hard: solar convection and diffusion mix material over billions of years. Even so, high-precision measurements of solar composition and of primitive meteorites continue to be explored as possible “fossils” of lost planets.
