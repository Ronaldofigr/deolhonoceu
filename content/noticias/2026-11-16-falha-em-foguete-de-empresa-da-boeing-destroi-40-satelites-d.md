Um foguete Vulcan da ULA (United Launch Alliance), empresa criada pela Boeing e pela Lockheed Martin, falhou, neste domingo (15.nov.2026), cerca de 10 minutos depois de decolar de Cabo Canaveral, na Flórida (EUA). O acidente destruiu a carga de 40 satélites da Amazon destinados à rede de internet Leo.

A falha encerra o histórico de 100% de sucesso da ULA desde sua fundação, em 2006. A empresa declarou que o veículo espacial partiu da Estação da Força Espacial de Cabo Canaveral, mas não alcançou a órbita.

“Com base nos dados recebidos pelo nosso diretor de lançamento, confirmamos que o veículo Vulcan, lançado do Complexo de Lançamento Espacial 41, sofreu uma anomalia 10 minutos após o início do voo e não conseguiu levar a missão Amazon à órbita”, declarou. A companhia disse que “ainda não tem dados específicos para entender o que aconteceu”.

A Amazon afirmou que o episódio faz parte dos riscos de operações inaugurais. “Qualquer lançamento envolve riscos, e isso é especialmente verdadeiro para missões inaugurais como a LV-01”, disse a gigante de tecnologia, em nota.

A empresa acrescentou: “A ULA tem sido uma parceira confiável da Amazon, e acreditamos que eles aprenderão com esta missão e cumprirão seus planos de longo prazo para o Vulcan”.

As causas do problema permanecem desconhecidas. Dados preliminares indicam que a falha ocorreu depois da separação entre o 1º estágio do foguete, impulsionado por motores principais BE-4, e o 2º estágio, que transportava os satélites. Os motores BE-4 são fabricados pela Blue Origin, empresa espacial de Jeff Bezos, fundador da Amazon. A Blue Origin colabora com a ULA na investigação do caso.

A Vulcan rocket of ULA (United Launch Alliance), a company created by Boeing and Lockheed Martin, failed on Sunday (15 Nov 2026) about 10 minutes after lifting off from Cape Canaveral, Florida (USA). The accident destroyed the payload of 40 Amazon satellites destined for the Leo internet network.

The failure ends ULA’s 100% success record since its founding in 2006. The company stated that the vehicle lifted off from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station but did not reach orbit.

“Based on data received by our launch director, we confirm that the Vulcan vehicle, launched from Space Launch Complex 41, experienced an anomaly 10 minutes after the start of flight and was unable to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit,” it said. The company added that it “does not yet have specific data to understand what happened.”

Amazon said the episode is part of the risks of inaugural operations. “Any launch involves risks, and that is especially true for inaugural missions such as LV-01,” the technology giant said in a statement.

The company added: “ULA has been a reliable partner of Amazon, and we believe they will learn from this mission and deliver on their long-term plans for Vulcan.”

The causes of the problem remain unknown. Preliminary data indicate that the failure occurred after separation between the rocket’s first stage, powered by BE-4 main engines, and the second stage, which carried the satellites. The BE-4 engines are manufactured by Blue Origin, the space company of Jeff Bezos, Amazon’s founder. Blue Origin is collaborating with ULA on the investigation.

Sunday’s flight was Vulcan’s 5th since its debut in 2024, and the first in 9 months. The rocket had gone through a period without launches because of technical investigations into its solid side boosters and the BE-4 engines.

Problems with the boosters had been recorded on the two previous flights, including a mission flown in February 2026 by the U.S. Space Force. Although those payloads reached orbit, the Pentagon was waiting for the result of the Amazon operation before authorizing the rocket to resume carrying national-security equipment.

The BE-4 engines also powered Blue Origin’s New Glenn rocket, which exploded on the launch pad in May 2026. The investigation attributed the accident to an engine failure, which led the manufacturer to modify the units delivered to ULA for the Vulcan program.

The expectation in the aerospace sector is of a lengthy investigation that could keep Vulcan out of operation for months. The standstill worsens the launch bottleneck in the U.S., already pressured by the imminent retirement of SpaceX’s Falcon 9.

The world’s main active rocket, Falcon 9 is no longer accepting new customer bookings.

The accident represents a new obstacle for Amazon’s plans to compete with Starlink, SpaceX’s satellite internet network that operates more than 11,000 spacecraft and serves millions of customers worldwide.

Unlike SpaceX, which uses its own rockets to place its satellites in orbit, Amazon depends on third-party suppliers and spends billions of dollars on launch contracts. In May, the explosion of the New Glenn rocket had already delayed the company’s schedule days before a planned Leo network launch.

Since 2025, Amazon has placed 398 Leo satellites in orbit through 14 launches. That volume corresponds to 12.3% of the 3,236 spacecraft needed to guarantee global coverage.

The company plans to start commercial operation of the internet service still in 2026. ULA was created in 2006 from the merger of Boeing’s Delta and Lockheed Martin’s Atlas rocket programs, which had recorded few failures in the decades before the merger.
