Earth has a large moon; Mars has two small ones; **Venus** has none. A new study proposes an explanation: Venus may once have had a satellite that was later **swallowed** or destroyed.

Dynamical models link the lack of a moon to Venus’s slow, retrograde rotation and to possible past collisions or tidal interactions. A satellite formed after a giant impact could have migrated inward and collided with the planet, or been ejected from the system.

The hypothesis fits the picture of terrestrial planets having chaotic histories of impacts and transient moons. Understanding why Venus ended up “moonless” helps compare the evolution of rocky worlds and interpret how often similar exoplanets host moons.
