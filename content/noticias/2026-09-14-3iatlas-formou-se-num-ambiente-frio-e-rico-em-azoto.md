A composição química, inferida a partir de observações espectroscópicas da coma e da cauda, aponta para condições de formação diferentes das típicas do disco protoplanetário solar. Estes resultados reforçam a ideia de que 3I/ATLAS é um visitante de outra região da Via Láctea, possivelmente ejetado de um sistema planetário distante há milhões ou milhares de milhões de anos. <!--lang:en-->

Astronomers have found new evidence that the interstellar comet **3I/ATLAS** — only the **third confirmed interstellar object** to enter the Solar System — formed in a **cold, nitrogen-rich** environment.

Chemical composition, inferred from spectroscopic observations of the coma and tail, points to formation conditions different from those typical of the solar protoplanetary disk. A relative excess of nitrogen-bearing species and the preservation of volatile ice suggest low temperatures and a chemistry distinct from “local” comets.

These results reinforce the idea that 3I/ATLAS is a visitor from another region of the Milky Way, possibly ejected from a distant planetary system millions or billions of years ago. Each interstellar object offers a rare sample of material formed in another stellar context — and 3I/ATLAS joins 1I/ʻOumuamua and 2I/Borisov as a natural laboratory to compare the chemistry of protoplanetary disks beyond the Sun.
