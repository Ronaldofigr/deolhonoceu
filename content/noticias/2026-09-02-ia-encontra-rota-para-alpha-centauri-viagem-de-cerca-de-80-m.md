The find came after a year of attempts by the team to find a route compatible with a small, solar-powered craft and a budget under $15 million.

## The solution came from AI

The challenge was to generate enough power without adding too much mass. Philip Johnston (Fermi Explorer co-founder) took the problem to Physical Superintelligence (PSI). The open-source *Get Physics Done* system broke the problem into tasks and chose which simulations to run. In about a week — with three days of nearly autonomous work — the tool proposed a mission profile the team had not considered.

The plan combines known orbital manoeuvres in a new way: the probe would slow into an orbit that takes it closer to the Sun than Mercury. The engine would fire on those passes; the higher insolation allows smaller panels and a lighter craft. The trajectory was presented in a paper not yet peer-reviewed.

## Low-cost interstellar mission

Alpha Centauri is about 4.4 light-years away. Fermi Explorer plans a 100–200 kg spacecraft, electric propulsion and at least 1 kg of payload (artistic and scientific materials, messages and a copy of the Voyager Golden Record).

“We don’t want to do another Breakthrough Starshot. We’re determined to do something that actually launches,” Johnston said. Unlike Starshot (laser sails at a fraction of light speed), Fermi Explorer relies on technologies considered available. The mission also evokes the Fermi paradox: sending a craft toward another star would already be a way to test one of the premises of that classic problem.
