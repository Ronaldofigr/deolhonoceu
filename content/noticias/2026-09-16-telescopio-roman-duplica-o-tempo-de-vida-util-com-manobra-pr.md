O Roman está a caminho do ponto de Lagrange L2, a cerca de 1,5 milhões de km da Terra, de onde mapeará vastas regiões do céu em infravermelho para estudar energia escura, matéria escura e exoplanetas por microlentes.

<!--lang:en-->

The **Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope**, launched in late August 2026, has just gained a dramatic extension of its science lifetime: a **high-precision engine burn** optimized propellant use and **doubled** the planned science lifetime of the mission (from about 11 to about **22 years** of science operations).

Roman is en route to the Sun–Earth L2 Lagrange point, about 1.5 million km from Earth, from where it will map vast regions of the sky in the infrared to study dark energy, dark matter and exoplanets via microlensing.

Every kilogram of fuel saved in the transfer and orbital-insertion phase translates into additional years of pointing and orbit corrections. The successful maneuver confirms propulsion-system performance and opens the door to a much longer observational legacy than the original baseline.

With its 300-megapixel Wide Field Instrument and coronagraph, Roman is positioned to become one of the pillars of the next decade of astronomy — now with a reinforced time margin.
